⚠ Critical Warning

Applying These Laws Inside an Organization

These laws frequently conflict with corporate ego, conventional wisdom, and internal politics. Knowing them is the easy part. Getting an organization to act on them is another matter entirely.

The Perception Problem

The Law of Perception runs counter to most corporate cultures where "being better" through benchmarking and quality programs is deeply ingrained. Management rarely welcomes any suggestion that challenges the better-product strategy.

The Focus Resistance

Organizations instinctively resist narrowing focus. Bright marketing teams feel compelled to expand and tinker. Proposing pruning is extremely difficult to sell to leadership who equate bigger with better.

The Sacrifice Paradox

Offering everything to everybody is deeply wired into most companies. Nobody wants to give up any customer segment, product line, or market opportunity — even when doing so would make them dramatically stronger.

The Line Extension Trap

The most dangerous law to challenge internally. Management believes successful brands have equity that can be stretched to cover any product. You won't find one board director in twelve willing to challenge this "basic truth."

The bottom line: If you violate the immutable laws, you risk failure in the marketplace. If you apply them, you risk being ignored or ostracized within your organization. Have patience — management changes, but the laws of marketing do not. Success is the best revenge.
