S1

The Secret Formula

Before you can grow any company, you must answer four fundamental questions. These questions took Russell years to discover — and a blank whiteboard at his lowest professional moment to finally articulate.

The Origin Story: Despite building a profitable company, Russell was miserable. He found himself wishing he had a boss who could fire him. When he finally sat down at a whiteboard to figure out why, he wrote two words: Who? Result? Those four questions eventually became The Secret Formula.

The Four Questions

Question #1
Who?

Who is your dream client? Not demographics — a specific, named avatar. Write out their characteristics. Find an actual photo. Put it on your wall. You'll spend more time with these people than your own family — choose carefully.

1
Question #2
Where?

Where can you find them? What Facebook groups? What email newsletters? What blogs? What keywords are they Googling? What books are they buying on Amazon? Answer this only after you know your WHO.

2
Question #3
Bait?

What bait will attract your dream client and repel everyone else? A physical book, CD, DVD, report — something your dream customer would pay for, offered free. Great bait filters: if a beginner wouldn't want it, you're on the right track.

3
Question #4
Result?

What result do you want to give them? Not what product — what transformation. A business is about the results you create for clients. Once they understand that, price is no longer a barrier.

4
The Avatar Exercise: Russell created two customer avatars — Julie and Mike. He listed their characteristics, then searched Google Images for photos matching those characteristics. He printed the photos and hung them on his wall. When you have a physical picture of your ideal customer, your perspective shifts completely.

S3

From a Ladder to a Funnel

A sales funnel is simply the online process that moves someone through the levels of your Value Ladder — providing value at each stage while making you profitable and identifying your dream clients.

In a perfect world, you'd convince every prospect to buy your $1M service immediately. But they haven't received value from you yet — and trust hasn't been built. A funnel solves this by doing two things simultaneously:

Goal 1: Provide Value

Serve each customer at the unique level of service they can afford — giving real value at every rung of the ladder.

Goal 2: Identify Buyers

Make money and stay profitable while naturally filtering out and finding dream clients who can afford your highest offers.

The Funnel Visualization

☁️  All Potential Traffic  — Prospects see your bait/ad
🎯 Bait / Frontend Offer — Some raise their hands
📦 Mid-Tier Product — Fewer buy, but deeper value
🎓 High-Ticket Offer — Committed buyers emerge
⭐ Dream Clients — Premium service candidates
The Depth Principle: The deeper your funnel and the more things you can offer clients, the more each customer is worth to you — and the more you can spend to acquire them. This is how you outbid competitors for every traffic source.
S4

How to Find Your Dream Customers

Before driving traffic, you must understand congregations — the online groups where your ideal customers already gather. The Internet makes it possible for any niche to congregate globally.

Prior to the Internet, five kids playing Magic: The Gathering in one high school were isolated from five others in another. Now they all congregate together online. If I have a product for Magic players, I just go find where they already are.
— Russell Brunson

Three Questions to Find Congregations

Q1: Who Is Your Target Market?

Go beyond demographics. What are their pains and passions? What do they think about? What were you like when you were in their position? Think back to when you were where they are now.

Q2: Where Are They Congregating?

What forums? What Facebook groups? What newsletters? What YouTube channels? What blogs? What keywords are they searching? What books are they buying? Do the research — it's worth every minute.

Q3: How Do You Get Them to Leave?

Use the Enquirer Interrupt — short, punchy, unusual headlines and images that stop the scroll. Model tabloid magazine techniques to grab attention and earn the click.

The Gold Mining Metaphor: The Internet is a mountain full of gold (your ideal customers). Your job is to find the vein. Once you find a small congregation, dig deeper — that vein runs far into the mountain. Finding the vein is the strategic work; mining it out is the tactical work you hire others to do.

🔍 Finding Your Congregation — Quick Method

  1. Go to Google and type your niche keywords + "forum"
  2. Search Facebook for groups related to your keywords
  3. Look at what newsletters people in your niche subscribe to
  4. Check Amazon for books your target market buys and read the reviews — those are the actual words your customers use
  5. Find YouTube channels they watch and read the comments
S5

The Three Types of Traffic

There are only three types of traffic. Understanding how they work — and how they connect — gives you the ability to direct the right traffic to the right offers and convert the maximum number into buyers.

Your One and Only Goal: Convert ALL traffic into traffic you OWN. That is how you grow your list and multiply your income without continuously paying for attention.
👑
Traffic You OWN

Your email list, podcast subscribers, social followers, blog readers. You send an email or post, you get instant traffic — no new ad spend. This is the most valuable.

Rule of thumb: ~$1/month per subscriber
🎛️
Traffic You CONTROL

Paid ads (Facebook, Google, YouTube), solo ads, banner ads, native ads, affiliates. You tell it where to go — but you pay for every click.

→ Always send to a squeeze page first
🌊
Traffic You DON'T Control

SEO, social shares, guest posts, YouTube views, PR. It shows up but you can't direct it. Includes: Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, search traffic.

→ Drive to a blog that functions as a squeeze page

Converting Traffic Into Traffic You Own

Controlled Traffic → Squeeze Page

All paid traffic goes to a squeeze page — a single-purpose page with one choice: give an email address or leave. No navigation, no distractions. Once they opt in, they become traffic you own.

Uncontrolled Traffic → Blog

The top third of your blog is a glorified squeeze page. Visitors who arrive organically land on a page where the dominant action is subscribing. Convert as many as possible before they leave forever.

Mark Joyner's Insight (Russell's first mentor): "Russell, you have to build a list." When Russell had 200 subscribers he made $200/month. 1,000 subscribers → $1,000/month. 10,000 subscribers → $10,000/month. Now with 500,000+ subscribers, the ratio holds. Once you understand that metric, list building becomes your highest priority.

S6

The Attractive Character

The missing link most business owners never create. The difference between making $1,000/month and $100,000/month often comes down entirely to whether you have an Attractive Character — and whether you've built it correctly.

An Attractive Character is the persona you share with your audience — the entity they follow, relate to, and buy from. It doesn't need to be the business owner. Jared made Subway a billion-dollar weight-loss brand. Joy Anderson made Russell's MicroContinuity launch produce a 50/50 male/female audience (usually 90% male).

The Four Elements

1
📖 Backstory

Where you came from. The further removed from where you are now, the more relatable. Without seeing your backstory, prospects can't follow you to where you are. They need to see that you were once where they are now.

2
🪄 Speaks in Parables

Little stories that illustrate relevant points. When Coach Schultz took Russell's wallet and money, then explained why — that parable taught the power of investment better than any lecture. Mine your life for parables.

3
💫 Character Flaws

Nobody wants to follow a perfect person — you can't relate. Superman becomes interesting only when kryptonite is introduced. Share your flaws. Be imperfect. Grammatical errors in emails aren't bugs — they're features.

4
⚡ Polarity

Being neutral is forgettable. Taking a strong stand splits your audience into fans and critics — both follow you passionately. People spend as much time sharing content they hate as content they love. Howard Stern built a media empire on this.

The Four Identities

Choose one primary identity. It shapes how you communicate, what stories you tell, and how your audience relates to you.

🗺️
The Leader

Has already achieved the result and leads the audience along the same path. Has experienced the pitfalls personally and can guide others around them.

🧭
The Adventurer

Goes on a journey to find the answer and brings back treasures to share. Doesn't always have all the answers — that curiosity is the appeal.

📰
The Reporter

Interviews experts and shares their wisdom. Great starting point when you're new to a niche — borrow others' credibility while building your own. (Russell's original identity.)

🦸
The Reluctant Hero

Doesn't want the spotlight but has information so important they're compelled to share it. Humble, moral-driven. Russell's current primary identity.

The Six Storylines

These are the plot structures you use repeatedly across emails, sales letters, videos, and social content. Learn to recognize and tell each one.

💔 Loss & Redemption
"I had everything. Then __ happened.
But it was a blessing because I learned __.
Now I __."
⚔️ Us vs. Them
Polarizes audience. Creates a rallying cry. Draws raving fans closer by identifying a common "enemy" — a mindset, system, or approach you oppose.
✨ Before & After
"First I was __.
Then I tried __.
Now I'm __."

Transformation stories. Often told with just pictures.
🔭 Amazing Discovery
"You're not going to believe what I just found! I hit it out of the park on my first try!" Creates excitement about a new solution being finally available.
🔐 Secret Telling
"I've got a secret... if you want to find out what it is, you need to __."

The lure of secrets pulls people up your Value Ladder.
🌟 Third-Person Testimonial
Sharing others' successes with your product/program. Provides social proof. Sprinkle liberally throughout all your stories.
S7

The Soap Opera Sequence

The moment someone joins your email list, you have a tiny window to build a bond before they forget who you are. The Soap Opera Sequence is a 5-email onboarding series that hooks new subscribers using the same story structure that keeps soap opera viewers glued to their screens for decades.

The key mechanic: open a loop in each email, then close it in the next. The reader is compelled to open the next email to find out what happens. Just like a soap opera cliffhanger keeps you watching through commercials.

Email #1
Set the Stage

A welcome email sent the moment they subscribe. Introduce yourself briefly. Tell them what to expect. Open a loop — hint at something amazing coming in the next email and tell them the exact subject line to watch for.

Email #2
Open with High Drama

Start at the point of maximum tension — don't begin at the beginning. "The gun was in my face..." Then go back and fill in the backstory. Lead up to a wall you hit (where they probably are now). Open a new loop — promise to share the solution tomorrow.

Email #3
The Epiphany

The "aha moment" that changed everything. You realized what the real problem was — and found the solution. This is where you introduce your core offer for the first time, tied directly to the epiphany. The offer solves the exact problem you identified.

Email #4
Hidden Benefits

Point out non-obvious benefits of knowing you / using your solution. "Sure, you'll make more money — but you'll also have freedom to vacation whenever you want." Hidden benefits often motivate action more than the obvious primary benefit. Use parables here.

Email #5
Urgency & CTA

The final push. Create real, legitimate urgency — a closing deadline, limited seats, expiring price. Fake urgency destroys credibility. There's always a real reason to act now — find it and use it. Clear call to action.

Technical note: The Soap Opera Sequence is an auto-responder — every new subscriber receives the same 5 emails in sequence, one per day. After Email #5, they move to the broadcast list where they receive your daily Seinfeld emails in real time.
S8

The Daily Seinfeld Sequence

After your Soap Opera Sequence, you send daily emails about… nothing. Just like the show. Entertainment first, offer second — and this counterintuitive approach dramatically outperforms content-heavy "value" emails.

My emails switched from 100% content to 90% entertainment and just 10% content, and my readership, opens, clicks, and sales all skyrocketed.
— Russell Brunson

The secret is simple: every entertaining story must tie back to something you're selling. If you just tell entertaining stories without tying in your products, you won't make a dime — even if you're the best storyteller alive. The story is the vehicle; the offer is the destination.

What to Write About

📖 Story Prompts

  • Something embarrassing that happened to you today
  • Something you bought recently that you regret
  • A crazy antics story about your kid or pet
  • Something that made you scream with rage yesterday (that you're laughing about today)
  • A funny thing from your past that teaches a lesson
  • Your vacation plans (and tie it to the freedom your product creates)

🔗 Tying to the Offer

  • Every story needs a bridge to a product or service
  • The connection can be direct or symbolic
  • The offer can be your own or an affiliate's
  • Frequency: ideally daily — Russell loses money every day he doesn't email
  • These are broadcasts, not auto-responders
  • Bonus: copy-paste to your blog for instant content
The Jiu-Jitsu Email — One of Russell's most successful Seinfeld emails started with "Jiu Jitsu is like wrestling for old, fat guys" and described how he was cutting 7 lbs before a tournament while his company was doing 6-figures in revenue that same week without him in the office. The story was about nothing — a tournament. The offer was high-end coaching. That email made over $100,000.

S9

Reverse Engineering a Successful Funnel

Before building anything, find someone already selling successfully to your target market and reverse-engineer everything they're doing. If the traffic already exists, why work to create it? Just put yourself in front of it.

The Golden Rule: Never start a new funnel with more than one unknown variable. Two unknowns = too much risk. Find the data before you build.

The Five Variables of Every Successful Campaign

① Demographics

Age, gender, income, location — but also psychographics: what they believe, fear, desire. Research your competitors' successful demographics to discover audiences you never considered.

② Offer

Buy your competitors' products. The first offer you see is just the bait — the real money is in the upsells and downsells. Map the entire funnel end to end.

③ Landing Page

The most important page in any funnel. What's working for competitors right now? Model it — don't reinvent. You can tweak and improve later, after you're profitable.

④ Traffic Source

What specific websites are your competitors advertising on? Banner ads, newsletters, native? Hint: ads that have been running for 6+ months are profitable — short-lived ads aren't.

⑤ Ad Copy

What images, headlines, and body copy are working? Why are people clicking? Model what's already converting instead of guessing blind. Collect winning ads in a swipe file.

The Reverse Engineering Process

1
List Direct & Indirect Competitors

Direct competitors sell the same thing to the same people. Indirect competitors sell something different to the same demographic. Both are goldmines of data.

2
Find Their Landing Page URLs

Search Google for your target keywords and click the paid ads — those advertisers are spending real money because it's working.

3
Research with SimilarWeb

Enter their URL to see traffic sources, demographics, which sites they advertise on, which ads are running, and how long each ad has been live. (Long-running ads = profitable ads.)

4
Buy Their Entire Funnel

Spend whatever it takes to see every upsell, downsell, and email sequence. Take detailed notes. This is critical competitive intelligence — worth every dollar spent.

5
Build Your Swipe File

Collect screenshots of ads, landing pages, emails, and funnels. Model what's working — never copy, but model the structure, format, and approach.

S10

The Seven Phases of a Funnel

The difference between a six-figure, seven-figure, and eight-figure business is whether you understand and optimize all seven phases. Improve any single phase and revenue increases — without adding a single new customer.

The Pre-Frame Principle: Each phase of the funnel pre-frames the next. Every step sets the emotional and cognitive context for what comes after it. The MIT Study: students who received a "warm" bio of a substitute professor rated him as good-natured and sociable. Students with the "cold" bio found the same professor irritable and ruthless. Same lecture. Same professor. Different frame = entirely different experience.
1
Determine Traffic Temperature
Is your traffic hot (knows you), warm (knows someone who knows you), or cold (no connection)? Each temperature requires different communication. Hot traffic gets personality. Warm gets a JV endorsement. Cold gets education first, then offer.
2
Set Up the Pre-Frame Bridge
The bridge before the landing page. For hot traffic: a short email. For warm traffic: a lift letter from the JV partner. For cold traffic: a full pre-frame page that educates them on the problem and solution vocabulary before they hit your offer page. This is the holy grail of online marketing.
3
Qualify Subscribers
Convert visitors into email subscribers via squeeze pages, opt-in forms, quiz funnels, or webinar registrations. If 1,000 people visit and 30% opt in, you have 300 qualified leads — people who actually want what you're offering.
4
Qualify Buyers
Immediately after someone subscribes, present a low-cost offer. Dan Kennedy's principle: "A buyer is a buyer is a buyer." Someone willing to pay once will keep paying — they're fundamentally different from a free subscriber. Find out who they are right now.
5
Identify Hyperactive Buyers
People who are in pain right now and will buy multiple things at once to solve it. Immediately offer upsells after any purchase. The window is brief — if they don't buy now, they forget and move on. Russell once bought multiple bowling products after losing to a coworker. Classic hyperactive buyer behavior.
6
Age and Ascend the Relationship
The first 5 phases happen in minutes. This phase happens over weeks and months. Continue providing value. Let them experience what they've purchased. Then introduce the next level of your Value Ladder through new funnels designed for warm/hot traffic.
7
Change the Selling Environment
High-ticket offers ($10K+) rarely close from a sales page alone. Move the sale to a phone conversation, a live event, or a direct mail piece. Changing the environment changes what's possible. A call center can sell what a landing page cannot.

S12

Frontend vs. Backend Funnels

Different funnels serve different traffic temperatures and different rungs on your Value Ladder. Using the wrong funnel for the wrong audience is like trying to close a $25,000 sale with a business card.

🌊 Cold Traffic

→ Frontend Funnels
  • Free + Shipping Funnel
  • Self-Liquidating Offer (SLO)
  • Continuity Funnel

These funnels can convert people who don't know you yet. They start with low-risk, high-value bait and qualify buyers immediately.

🌡️ Warming Traffic

→ Middle Funnels
  • Perfect Webinar Funnel
  • Invisible Funnel Webinar
  • Product Launch Funnel

After buyers experience your frontend value and bond with the Attractive Character, they're ready for mid-ticket products ($297–$2,000).

🔥 Hot Traffic

→ Backend Funnels
  • High-Ticket 3-Step Application
  • Phone sales (setter/closer)
  • Live event close

Hot prospects know you, trust you, and have experienced your value. Change the selling environment. Move the sale to a phone call or live event.

The Best Bait: Free + Shipping

The discovery that took Russell from $30,000/year to seven figures in 18 months. The word FREE is the most powerful word in marketing — and structuring your bait correctly can get 8x more customers while doubling revenue from the same traffic.

Russell ran the same product offer two ways and compared the results across hundreds of thousands of visitors:

Why This Works: 8% take the free+shipping offer (vs 1% for the direct $197 offer). Then 25% of those buyers take the $197 upsell — because their credit card is already out, they've already committed psychologically, and the first "yes" makes the second "yes" dramatically easier. Result: nearly 2x the revenue with 8x more customers entering your funnel.

Why Physical Beats Digital

📦 Physical Product

Lets you use the word FREE while requiring credit card info for shipping — qualifying the buyer. Enables one-click upsells on the next page. Higher perceived value than a download.

💳 The Order Form Bump

Adding a $37 checkbox after credit card info but before Submit: consistently ~34% of buyers add it. Zero extra pages, zero extra selling. This alone can match the revenue of your entire frontend offer.

🎯 Applying to Any Business

Coaches: Free book or CD. eCommerce: Free sample or low-cost accessory. Network Marketing: Free "how I find leads" DVD. Offline: Free report or physical kit that solves their biggest problem.

Invisible Funnel Webinar
Warm Traffic

Participants register with a credit card but pay nothing upfront. They attend a 3–4 hour premium training. Only after the webinar, if they loved it, they do nothing and get charged. If they didn't, they email before midnight and pay nothing. Builds massive goodwill and trust.

Registration page: Magic Bullet script (try before you buy)
Confirmation (credit card held, not charged)
3–4 hour premium content webinar
Soft sell to high-ticket application at end
Auto-charge next day (cancel window closes)
Real Results: 550 registrants → 85% attended (vs 30% for free webinars) → 90% kept → $23,500 from webinar price alone → additional 6 figures from coaching applications over 30 days.

Funnel #6 — Mid Value Ladder
Product Launch Funnel
Warm/Hot Traffic

Made famous by Jeff Walker. Split the sales presentation across 4 videos delivered over several days. Each video delivers real value while building desire for the final offer. Works best with your own list or a JV partner's list.

Video #1: "Wow & How" — Big idea + proof it works
Video #2: "Transformational Education" — Show them how
Video #3: "Ownership Experience" — Imagine having this
Video #4: "The Offer" — Here's what I've got + CTA
The Hook: Each video ends with a hook that creates desire for the next video. By the time Video #4 (the offer) arrives, prospects have spent 3+ days consuming content and are primed to buy. The sale almost makes itself.

Funnel #7 — Backend
High-Ticket, Three-Step Application
Hot Traffic Only

Replaced a 60-person call center with 2 salespeople producing nearly the same revenue. The application process pre-qualifies prospects AND pre-sells them on why they should work with you — before anyone picks up a phone.

Step 1: Case Study Video

Simple page with a video showing client results. After watching, they click "Apply Now" — entered with the frame that they're applying to be accepted, not that you're pitching them.

Step 2: Application Page

Full application form. Serves two purposes: gives salespeople data on the prospect AND gets the prospect to sell you on why you should work with them. Massive psychological shift. Weeds out non-buyers instantly.

Step 3: Homework Page

3 videos to watch while waiting for the call: (1) How to succeed in the program, (2) Russell's personal story (builds deep trust), (3) Case studies and testimonials. By the time the closer calls, the prospect is already sold.

The Setter/Closer System: Two separate salespeople. The setter qualifies the prospect, finds their emotional hot buttons, gets four commitments (time, coachability, starting today, investing with OPM). Then the closer calls back to magnify the pain and get the prospect to sell themselves on why they'll succeed. When the prospect is telling YOU why they need the program — the sale is made.

Your Implementation Action Plan

Feeling overwhelmed is good — it means your brain is making connections. Here is the exact sequence Russell recommends for implementation.

1
Define Your Dream Client

Answer the Secret Formula: Who, Where, Bait, Result. Create two customer avatars. Find actual photos and put them on your wall. Do NOT skip this — every subsequent decision flows from knowing exactly who you serve.

2
Create Your Bait Quickly — Don't Overthink It

What one thing could you give away (free + shipping) that would attract your dream client and repel everyone else? Start there. It can be a book, a CD, a DVD, a physical sample. Done is better than perfect.

3
Map Your Complete Value Ladder

What do you have at each level — bait, frontend, continuity, mid-ticket, high-ticket? What's missing? Most businesses have 1–2 rungs and stall. The missing rungs are where the growth lives. Add them.

4
Build Your First Funnel (Start Simple)

Build a Two-Step Free + Shipping funnel or an SLO funnel. Write your first Soap Opera Sequence (5 emails). Set up daily Seinfeld broadcasting. Build the Attractive Character first — then the funnel will convert.

5
Add Depth — One Funnel at a Time

After your first funnel is working, add a webinar funnel for warm traffic. Then a product launch. Then a high-ticket application. Each new funnel unlocks the next level of your Value Ladder and your revenue ceiling.
