Section 01

The Humor Cliff

Why, somewhere between kindergarten and the corner office, we lose our laughter — and what that costs us.

Frequency of Laughter Across a Lifetime

Self-reported daily laughter / smiling, by age. Notice the cliff right around age 23.

~300 laughs per day for the average 4-year-old. They find the world inherently absurd, surprising, and delightful.
~300 / 2.5 mo for the average 40-year-old. By midlife, that magical-thinking laughter has slowed to a trickle.

When we enter the workforce, we're often handed an unspoken rule: professional means serious. We mute the goofy parts of ourselves the moment we step into the office — and pay for it in trust, creativity, and joy.

Section 02

Four Deadly Myths About Humor

Tap each card to flip and debunk.

01
The Serious Business Myth

"Humor has no place in serious work."

Tap to debunk →

Wrong: humor IS the work.

Leaders who use humor are seen as 27% more motivating and 23% more admired. Their teams are more bonded and report higher job satisfaction. Seriousness about the work and lightness in delivery are not opposites.

02
The Failure Myth

"If nobody laughs, I've failed."

Tap to debunk →

Appropriate > Funny.

The goal isn't laughs — it's signaling humanity. Aim for the safe quadrants:

JACKPOT
funny + appropriate
STILL GOOD
not funny + appropriate
VILLAIN
not funny + inappropriate
JERK
funny + inappropriate
03
The Being Funny Myth

"You have to be funny."

Tap to debunk →

You don't. You just have to signal.

You don't need to land a punchline. You just need to communicate that you have a sense of humor — by laughing generously, calling out absurdity, or simply not taking yourself too seriously. The bar is much, much lower than you think.

04
The Born With It Myth

"Humor is innate, not learned."

Tap to debunk →

Funny is a muscle.

Comedians train. Improvisers practice. So can you. Humor is a skill built through observation, structure, and a growth mindset. The more you exercise it — noticing the absurd, riffing safely, embracing failure — the stronger it gets.

Section 03

Your Brain on Humor

Four scientifically-grounded reasons levity is your most underrated leadership skill.

⚡

Power

Humor signals confidence, status, and competence — fast.

  • Speakers who used humor were perceived as 37% higher in status.
  • In negotiation, a small joke at offer time produced 18% higher closing prices.
  • Material delivered with humor is up to 2× more memorable.
🤝

Bonds

Shared laughter is the fastest accelerator of trust.

  • Laughter triggers a release of oxytocin, the "trust hormone."
  • Pairs who laughed together reported 30% more intimate connection.
  • Couples sharing laughter report 23% more relationship satisfaction.
💡

Creativity

Humor unlocks the cognitive flexibility creativity requires.

  • Subjects primed with humor solved the candle problem at 2× the rate.
  • Trained comedians produced 20% more ideas, rated 25% more creative.
  • Laughter slashes cortisol by ~39%, freeing the brain to wander.
🛡️

Resilience

Humor is a sword and a shield against adversity.

  • Cortisol down ~39%; epinephrine down ~70%.
  • One longevity study: 48% lower all-cause mortality risk for women with high humor.
  • Humor reframes pain — and reframes are how we recover.

Section 05

The Anatomy of Funny

Two principles, two crafts: how to find the funny, and how to form it.

Principle 01

Truth is at the heart of humor.

The best humor isn't invented — it's noticed. It's the recognition of a truth, articulated in a way the audience hadn't yet put into words. If it doesn't ring true, it doesn't land.

Principle 02

Humor requires surprise & misdirection.

Set the audience down a path, then pivot. The laugh lives in the gap between the expected and the actual. No surprise, no spark.

Finding the Funny

Five entry points for spotting humor in everyday life.

🔍

Incongruity

Notice the contradictions, mismatches, and "wait, what?" moments around you.

❤️

Emotion

What do you love? What do you hate? Strong feelings are comedy goldmines.

💭

Opinion

Strongly-held views — even silly ones — give your humor a point of view.

😬

Pain

The mundane indignities. The cringe. The shared suffering. (Comedy = tragedy + time.)

😊

Delight

What makes you smile? Wonder, beauty, weird coincidences — all entry points.

Forming the Funny

Six time-tested techniques used by the pros.

📈

Exaggerate

Push the truth past plausible. "I have a thousand emails. Literally a thousand."

⚖️

Create Contrast

Pair the high and low. The grand and the petty. Tuxedo with sneakers.

🎯

Use Specifics

"A snack" isn't funny. "A single, soft Werther's Original" is. Specifics paint pictures.

🔗

Make Analogies

"Our roadmap is a Jenga tower someone's about to sneeze on." Comparison + collision.

3️⃣

The Rule of Three

Two to set the pattern, one to break it. "Liberty, equality, snacks."

🌍

Build Out the World

Sustain a premise. Add detail. Let the audience live there with you for a beat.

Section 06

Humor at Work

Practical, low-risk ways to inject levity into the daily mechanics of professional life.

Communication

Write Like a Human

  • Drop callbacks into emails (reference an in-joke from earlier)
  • Spice up your sign-offs ("Stay caffeinated," "With moderate enthusiasm,")
  • Add a P.S. — humans love a P.S.
  • Make your out-of-office reply a tiny piece of theater
Difficult Moments

Levity Under Pressure

  • Saying hard things with warmth, not flippancy
  • Acknowledging your own mistakes openly and with humor
  • Giving tough feedback wrapped in care
  • Persuading without pushing — humor lowers defenses
Team Rituals

Break the Ice

  • Icebreakers people actually want to participate in
  • Tone-setters at the top of meetings
  • Cold opens that signal "this won't be a death march"
  • "Bad idea" brainstorms that unlock real ones

Four Leadership Principles

  • Humanize YourselfShow the person behind the title — your hobbies, your weird snacks, your messy tabs.
  • Self-Deprecate WiselyPunch up or in, never down. Mock your power, your role, your blunders — never others'.
  • Balance Gravity & LevityThe strongest leaders carry both. Gravity earns respect; levity earns affection.
  • Own Mistakes Openly"Well, I just learned that one the expensive way" beats a defensive memo every time.
Section 08

Building a Culture of Levity

Spot the people who already carry the spark — then build the systems that fan the flame.

🌪️

The Instigators

Rabble-rousers who challenge a too-rigid culture. The Johnny Damon types who walk into a buttoned-up clubhouse and start a beard-growing contest. Disruptors of the staid.

⭐

Culture Carriers

Rising stars whose humor is openly part of their professional brand. They model that levity and excellence are compatible — even mutually reinforcing.

💎

Hidden Gems

Quiet high performers with unexpected wit. Give them a small stage — an offsite skit, a Slack channel — and watch the whole team light up.

Four Ways to Institutionalize Levity

Honor the Peak-End Rule

People remember the peaks and the endings of an experience — not the average. Engineer great peaks. Engineer better endings.

Turn Accidents Into Folklore

The blooper from last year's all-hands? Make it a recurring callback. Mishaps become rituals. Rituals become culture.

Let Space Speak

Walls, mugs, signs, Slack emoji — the physical and digital environment is constantly broadcasting "what we are." Make sure it's broadcasting humanity.

Adopt "Yes, And"

The improv mantra: accept what's offered, build on it. It transforms meetings from tug-of-war into something generative.

Fun is not a top-down thing. — Ed Catmull, co-founder, Pixar
Section 09

Navigating the Gray Areas

Three ingredients live inside every joke — and the ratio is what separates a laugh from a lawsuit.

T

Truth

What the audience recognizes — the universal "yes, that."

P

Pain

Discomfort, taboo, friction — the source of the spark.

D

Distance

Time, geography, identity — how close the subject sits to the audience.

The Humor Fail Lifecycle

1

Recognize

Real laughter rolls; polite laughter clips. Status inflates laughter — the more powerful you are, the more pity-laughs you'll get. Read past them.

2

Diagnose

Was it a misread room? A punch-down? Wrong medium (Slack vs. in-person)? A context switch nobody followed? Name the specific failure.

3

Make It Right

A sincere apology + visible action. As Bagdonas says: "Get out the power washer." Don't sweep it under the rug — clean it up in public.

⚠️

The One Inviolable Rule

Never make another person's identity the prop, the plot point, or the punch line. Punch up at power. Punch in at yourself. Never punch down.

Humor as a Secret Weapon in Life

Hospice nurse Bronnie Ware named the five regrets of the dying. Humor speaks to every one of them.

🦁

Boldness

"I wish I had lived more fearlessly."

Humor is permission to take the risk, drop the script, jump in.

"I'd rather regret the things I've done than regret the things I haven't done." — Lucille Ball
🌿

Authenticity

"I wish I'd lived true to myself."

Your sense of humor is a fingerprint. Sharing it is sharing you.

"Do your thing, and don't care if they like it." — Tina Fey
🎯

Presence

"I wish I'd appreciated the moment more."

Laughter only exists in the now. It pulls you back into the room.

"The more relaxed you are, the better you are at everything." — Bill Murray
☀️

Joy

"I wish I had laughed more."

Joy isn't an accident that happens to you. It's a daily decision.

"Find out who you are and do it on purpose." — Ellen DeGeneres-style sentiment, attributed widely
❤️

Love

"I wish I had said 'I love you' more."

Where there is laughter, love trails close behind. Both deserve to be spoken.

"Falling in love is the great delusion that makes you happy." — Stephen Colbert (paraphrased)
