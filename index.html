The signature tool

The Lenses

A lens is a perspective — a focused set of questions to ask about your game. The book introduces 112 of them; each works best when used alongside others. Together they form a designer's toolbox.

LENS #1
The Lens of Emotion

People may forget what you said, but they'll never forget how you made them feel. Use this lens to make sure the emotions you create are the right ones.

  • What emotions would I like my player to experience? Why?
  • What emotions are players (including me) having when they play now? Why?
  • How can I bridge the gap between the emotions players are having and the emotions I'd like them to have?

The core idea

A game is a problem-solving activity, approached with a playful attitude.

That definition — arrived at after a careful chase through toys, play, fun, and games — sets the agenda for everything that follows. Game designers don't really make games. They make experiences. Games are the machines that produce them.

We cannot create experiences directly. We create artifacts — rule sets, game boards, computer programs — and cross our fingers that the experience that takes place during interaction is something the player will enjoy. Jesse Schell
Where to start

If you're new here

A few suggested paths through the material, depending on what you need today.

I want to design my first game

Start with Foundations, then Process to learn how to iterate. Skim Mechanics for the vocabulary.

I want to make my game better

Read Mechanics (especially Balance), then Experience Design. Run every lens you can over your prototype.

I want to understand why games matter

Start with The Experience, then jump to Impact — how games change us, and the designer's responsibility.
