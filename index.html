About the Book

In "Ogilvy on Advertising," David Ogilvy shares his invaluable insights and experiences in an engaging and straightforward manner. The book covers how to land a job in advertising, select the right agency, and craft compelling copy that captures attention. Ogilvy reveals the secrets behind successful campaigns, presents groundbreaking research findings, and explores advertising's role for charitable organizations.


With his signature blend of honesty and generosity, Ogilvy offers readers a treasure trove of knowledge. Intended for both novices and seasoned professionals seeking to enhance their effectiveness, the book focuses on what Ogilvy knows best: selling.

👤

About David Ogilvy

Born on June 23, 1911, in West Horsley, England, Ogilvy was a pioneering figure in advertising. Educated at Fettes College and Christ Church, Oxford — though he never completed his degree — his career began in the kitchen of the Hotel Majestic in Paris, where he cultivated skills in discipline and management.


He excelled in door-to-door sales for Aga Cookers, earning acclaim for a sales manual Fortune magazine called "probably the best sales manual ever written." In 1938, he emigrated to the United States and joined George Gallup's Audience Research Institute, adopting rigorous research methodologies. In 1949, he founded Ogilvy & Mather, which grew into one of the largest agencies globally.

Chapter 2 Deep Dive

How to Produce Advertising That Sells

Ogilvy's foundational principles for creating effective advertising, drawn from decades of empirical research and real campaign results.

Principle 01

Do Your Homework

Successful advertising begins with thorough research about the product and market. "You don't stand a tinker's chance of producing successful advertising unless you start by doing your homework." Understand features, competition, and consumer perceptions first.

Principle 02

Positioning Is Everything

Positioning defines how a product is perceived in the market. Identify unique attributes and align them with target consumer needs. Effective positioning creates enduring campaigns by answering "What does this product do, and for whom?"

Principle 03

Build a Strong Brand Image

"Every advertisement should be thought of as a contribution to the brand image." Consistent messaging and a positive image influence consumer perceptions more than product features alone. Quality aspirational branding drives choices.

Principle 04

Find the Big Idea

"It takes a big idea to attract the attention of consumers." These aren't easily generated — the creative process involves informing the mind and allowing subconscious thoughts to flow freely. A true big idea must work for thirty years.

Principle 05

Simplicity & Product Focus

Make the product the hero. Consumers respond better to straightforward, product-centric messages rather than flashy or convoluted campaigns. Simplify the message — don't let creative ego obscure what you're selling.

Principle 06

Be Positively Good

Instead of claiming superiority over competitors, focus on being "positively good." Achieve this through clear, honest communication of product benefits. This builds consumer confidence and encourages selection based on perceived quality.

Principle 07

Repeat What Works

Successful ads should be repeated until they lose effectiveness. Research shows repeated exposure does not diminish readership and can continually attract new consumers. Don't abandon a winning campaign for the sake of novelty.

Principle 08

Avoid Committees

Committee-driven campaigns result in diluted messages. "The average time it takes them to produce a campaign is 117 days — faster than goats but slower than hyenas." Strong creative ideas should not be bogged down by excessive feedback.

Principle 09

Generate Word of Mouth

Effective advertising can become part of popular culture, generating spontaneous consumer conversations. Creating memorable campaigns that embed themselves into daily life leads to organic promotion beyond the paid placement.
