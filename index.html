The Oracle of Wisdom

Three Thousand Years of Inquiry

The I Ching is among the oldest of the Chinese classical texts — a manual of divination, a philosophical compendium, and a living commentary on the patterns of change. Through sixty-four hexagrams, it maps the cosmos of human situations and counsels how the wise might move within them.

01

Two Primal Forces

All things arise from the interplay of two opposing yet complementary principles: yang, the strong and light-giving, and yin, the yielding and receptive. Solid and broken lines render this polarity visible.

02

Eight Trigrams

Three lines stacked form a trigram — heaven, earth, thunder, water, mountain, wind, fire, lake. These eight figures encode the elemental forces shaping the natural world and human affairs.

03

Sixty-Four Hexagrams

Each pair of trigrams creates a hexagram — six lines describing a moment in time, a situation, a tendency. Together they form a complete map of the situations of life and the way through them.

04

The Judgment & The Image

Each hexagram carries a Judgment — guidance for action — and an Image — a model of how the sage should embody the situation. Each of the six lines offers further counsel.

The Foundation

Yin & Yang

The Receptive does not combat the Creative, but completes it. Between them, all forms arise.

Yang ━━━━━

The Creative

Light-giving, active, strong, of the spirit. Its image is heaven; its movement is unbroken; its quality is the power that begins all things. The solid line represents the primal energy that initiates and persists.

━━ ━━ Yin

The Receptive

Dark, yielding, devoted. Its image is earth; its quality the maternal that brings to birth what the Creative begets. The broken line represents the receptive vessel — productive of good when led, productive of evil when it usurps.

The Eight Forces

The Trigrams

Three lines, each yin or broken, yang or solid — eight in all. Heaven and earth, thunder and water, mountain and wind, fire and lake. These are the elemental atoms of the Book of Changes.
